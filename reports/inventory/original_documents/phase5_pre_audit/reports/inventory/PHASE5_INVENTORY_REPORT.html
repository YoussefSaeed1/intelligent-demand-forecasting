Phase 5: Inventory simulation review

Hypothetical lost-sales simulation; sales are a potentially censored demand proxy. Full populations: Store Item 500 and M5 30,490 series at each of four original validation origins; 28 days per episode. Origins reset stock. Saved forecasts are fixed at issue, not daily refreshed. These validation windows also informed Phase 4 selection; comparisons are exploratory and validation-reused, with no significance claim. Costs are normalised hypothetical units (NCU), not realised savings.

Scope and assumptions

Base: L=7 full intervening days; R=1; buffer=3 training-mean days; initial=7 days; h=1 NCU/unit/day, p=10 NCU/unmet unit, k=1 NCU/order. Empty initial pipeline. End-day orders arrive start t+L+1. All policies stop new orders when the remaining L+R forecast dates are unavailable; remaining demand is still scored.

17 predefined scenarios × 8 dataset/origin cohorts × 4 policies = 544 completed cases. No final holdout demand was used. Buffers and initial stocks use trailing 56-day origin-available means, not same-window errors. No target service probability is calibrated.

Higher forecast accuracy need not minimise an asymmetric finite-window inventory objective. Cost rankings depend on holding/shortage trade-offs, starting stock, pipeline timing, buffer and end-of-horizon cutoff. Four origins and repeated series are dependent. No confidence interval or significance test is asserted.

Limits and reproducibility

Unlimited deterministic supply; no observed stock, supplier constraints, calibrated costs or latent demand. No backorders. Finite episodes with reset inventory and identical late-order cutoff do not establish steady-state operational performance. Variability stress can change mean and zero frequency as well as variance. No realised savings or deployment recommendation follows.
