Research objective and datasets

Daily H28 point forecasting on Store Item (500 series; 913,000 historical rows; 2013–2017) and M5 (30,490 series; 59,181,090 historical rows; 2011–2016). Assess whether compact global ML and statistical candidates improve over simple baselines under origin-bounded information. All comparisons are full-population.

Search, metrics and training design

The four original expanding validation origins are unchanged. A seed-42 finite random search evaluated 23 tuning configurations and four unchanged references, each on four origins. Primary selection is the equal-origin mean of equal-series MAE. Secondary errors use full-history seasonal-7 training scales; pooled RMSE and WAPE aggregate squared/absolute errors and actual totals. No early stopping, adaptive budget expansion, holdout screening or population mismatch was used. Store Item families: LightGBM, Random Forest, Holt-Winters; references: Seasonal Naive and Seasonal Moving Average. M5: price-free RF/LightGBM and TSB; references: SN and MA28.

Interpretation and threats to validity

Observed sales may be constrained by availability and are not latent demand. M5 price publication latency is unverified: every Phase 4 ML candidate is price-free; Phase 3 price-enabled M5 scores remain conditional research references. SNAP, events and future prices are excluded. Equal seeded series/time-stratified ML sampling uses 150,000 Store Item labels and 182,940 M5 labels per origin, with no outcome filtering; all series and zero-demand histories remain eligible. This is 300 and 6 labels per series respectively, not full-history supervised training. Four validation windows and one final 28-day window cover limited seasonal regimes. Search-selected validation gains are optimistic estimates after model selection. Origin spread is descriptive variability, not a confidence interval. Correlated series and only four origins do not establish statistical significance. Grouped leaf errors by hierarchy are not aggregate-node forecasts or official WRMSSE. Undefined scale/actual denominators stay null and their coverage is reported. Accuracy does not demonstrate inventory savings or economic superiority. Runtime is measured worker cost with verified cache reuse; hardware load and cache warmth vary, so these observations are not controlled hardware benchmarks. Direct cross-dataset transfer is untested; compare methodology and relative baseline performance rather than absolute sales-unit errors. Historical Phase 3 statistical subset results cannot establish population performance; Phase 4 comparisons use the complete approved populations. Holdout results cannot be used for retuning. An independent Claude audit is still mandatory after Phase 4 and before Phase 5; it must review scientific integrity without redesigning models to fit the holdout.
